2026 PMP Exam Structure: Sections, Breaks, and Question Types Explained

PMI's outline says one thing and test-takers report another. Here is exactly how the 2026 exam is laid out, the sections, the breaks, the question types, and where the confusion comes from.

The Quick Answer

The 2026 PMP exam is 180 questions (170 scored, 10 unscored pretest) in 240 minutes with two 10-minute breaks. It runs as a case-study section first, then an independent-question portion. The first break comes after the case-study section, and the second falls midway through the independent-question portion. You cannot return to a section once its break starts. PMI's line about questions being placed at random is about the pretest items, not the section order, and there is no fixed number of case studies per form.

If you have read r/pmp lately, you have seen the confusion. One person says the case studies come first, there is a break after them, and you cannot go back. Someone else says the questions are placed at random. Both camps are half right, and the gap between what PMI documents and what test-takers report is exactly what trips people up. Here is how the 2026 PMP exam is actually structured, straight from PMI's Exam Content Outline, with the reported-versus-documented tension spelled out so you can prep without guessing.

The 2026 PMP exam at a glance

The exam is 180 questions in 240 minutes. Of those 180, 170 are scored and 10 are unscored pretest questions that do not affect your result. That is four hours, and it works out to roughly 80 seconds per question if you spread the time evenly, though you will not, because the case studies eat more of it. There are two 10-minute breaks. The tutorial and survey at the start and end are optional and do not come out of your 240 minutes.

The section structure and the two breaks

This is the part people get wrong. The exam is not one flat pile of 180 questions you move through however you like. PMI's outline describes a case-study section and then an independent-question portion, with the breaks falling between them. The first 10-minute break comes right after the case-study section. The second comes roughly midway through the independent-question portion. The rule that catches people: once you start a break, you cannot return to the questions from the section you just finished. So when you leave the case studies, they are closed. Plan your review time before you hit that break, not after.

The confusion: "randomly placed" versus a front-loaded case block

Here is where the Reddit arguments come from. PMI's outline also says, in the same section, that all questions are randomly placed throughout the exam. Read quickly, that sounds like it contradicts the "case studies first" structure. It does not. That line sits in the paragraph about pretest questions, and it reads most naturally as how the 10 unscored items are scattered among the scored ones so you cannot tell which is which. It is not saying the case studies and independent questions are shuffled together. The section-and-break structure described a few lines later is explicit. As for the person who said "break after 40," that is one candidate's form, not a rule. PMI states the exact number of items for each question type may vary by form, so there is no fixed count of case studies, and no fixed number of questions per case.

The question types you will see

The 2026 exam uses more than plain multiple choice. Expect multiple-choice single response, multiple response with more than one correct answer, drag-and-drop, matching, point-and-click hotspots, and pull-down lists. Two types are flagged new for 2026: Case or Scenario, where you read a detailed situation, sometimes with a chart, and answer a set of questions on it, and Graphic-Based, where you interpret a chart, diagram, or image to answer. Those two show up on every delivery format. A few of the others, like point-and-click, matching, and pull-down, only appear on the computer-based test.

What this means for how you prep

Three things follow from the structure. First, practice full case sets, not just single questions, so the front-loaded case block does not rattle you. Frequently asked questions

How is the 2026 PMP exam structured?

The exam is 180 questions (170 scored, 10 unscored pretest) in 240 minutes. It runs as a case-study section first, then an independent-question portion, with two 10-minute breaks between and no returning to a section once its break has started.

Do the case studies come first on the 2026 PMP exam, and can you go back?

Yes. PMI's outline puts the first break right after the case-study section, so the case studies sit in one block at the start. Once you begin that break, you cannot return to those questions.

Why do some people say the 2026 PMP exam questions are randomly placed?

PMI's outline does say all questions are randomly placed throughout the exam, but that line is about how the 10 unscored pretest questions are mixed in with the scored ones. It does not override the case-study section and break structure described in the same place.

How long is the 2026 PMP exam and how many breaks are there?

It is 240 minutes with two 10-minute breaks. The first falls after the case-study section, the second roughly midway through the independent-question portion. The tutorial and survey are optional and are not counted in the 240 minutes.
