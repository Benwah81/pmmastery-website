Brain Sensei vs The PM PrepCast: Which Should You Pick for 2026?

August 11, 2026 10 min read Platform Comparison

Brain Sensei and The PM PrepCast show up in the same searches and the same "best PMP course" lists, so it is fair to treat them as direct rivals. They are. Both are self-paced online courses, both give you the 35 contact hours you need to apply for the exam, both are updated for the 2026 format, and neither is a PMI Authorized Training Partner. The real difference is how they teach and how you pay: Brain Sensei is an animated, story-driven course sold on a subscription, and PM PrepCast is lecture-style video sold once with a bigger stack of mock exams. This is an honest head-to-head from someone building a competing practice tool, so I will tell you where each one wins and where a question bank fits after you have picked.

Full disclosure up front: I am a current PMP candidate building PM Mastery, which is a practice platform, not a course. That means Brain Sensei and PM PrepCast are not my direct competitors, they do a job PM Mastery does not do, so I have no reason to tilt this comparison. I will point out at the end where PM Mastery does fit, and you can judge that on its own.

The one thing they have in common

Before comparing them, settle the question that trips up most candidates. PMI requires 35 contact hours of project management education before you can apply for the PMP exam. Both Brain Sensei and PM PrepCast deliver those 35 hours and issue a certificate. That certificate is what counts toward your application.

Here is the part people get wrong: neither course is a PMI Authorized Training Partner. PMI replaced its old Registered Education Provider program with the Authorized Training Partner (ATP) program in 2021, and the ATP program does not accept self-paced online courses. So no self-directed online course carries the ATP badge, PM PrepCast and Brain Sensei included. That is not a knock on either one. You do not need an ATP course to qualify. Any legitimate 35-hour course satisfies PMI's education requirement, and both of these do. The only place the ATP badge matters is some employer reimbursement policies, so if your company is paying, check its rules before you buy.

Brain Sensei at a glance

Brain Sensei is a self-paced PMP course built around an animated storyline set in feudal Japan. That sounds gimmicky until you use it and realize the story is the point: the format forces you to interact with the material instead of passively watching, which is how it gets the content to stick. The 2026 version is rebuilt for the new exam and PMBOK 8.

What you get:

  • 35 contact hours and the certificate you need to apply
  • A highly interactive, story-based course you click through, not a lecture you watch
  • An exam simulator with 2,200+ practice questions
  • A spaced repetition system for retaining terminology
  • A 100% pass guarantee (retake the course free if you meet the requirements and still fail) and a 30-day refund window
  • 1 year of access

Pricing, as of mid-2026, is around $349.99 for a year or $99.99 per month. The monthly option only makes sense if you are going to finish fast, since a slow six-month run on the monthly plan costs more than the annual.

The PM PrepCast at a glance

The PM PrepCast, run by Cornelius Fichtner and OSP International, has been around since 2008 and has trained more than 60,000 candidates. It is the more traditional of the two: structured video lessons you work through module by module, closer to a classroom course delivered online. The 2026 version is aligned to the current Exam Content Outline and references PMBOK 8.

What you get in the Elite bundle:

  • 35 contact hours from the video course, plus the certificate
  • Lecture-style video training, a large library of module-based lessons
  • The PrepCast Exam Simulator with 1,920 questions, including four full-length 180-question exams and a separate quiz pool
  • 90 days of simulator access that only starts counting when you take your first exam, with up to a year to activate
  • 1 year of access to the course videos
  • A 30-day money-back guarantee with conditions

PrepCast sells in tiers. The course-only Basic tier is around $279, the Elite bundle (course plus simulator) is around $349 as a one-time payment, and an Elite PLUS tier adds a formula guide and a week-by-week study plan. Unlike Brain Sensei, there is no subscription: you pay once.

Teaching style is the real fork

This is the decision that actually matters, and it comes down to how you learn. The contact hours, the 2026 alignment, and the roughly matched price are close enough that they do not break the tie. The teaching format does.

Brain Sensei is built for people who lose focus in a straight lecture. The animated story, the click-through interactions, and the spaced repetition are all there to keep you engaged and to force recall, which is genuinely useful if you have ever opened a video course and realized twenty minutes later that you absorbed nothing. The tradeoff is that the cartoon framing is not for everyone. Some candidates find it charming, some find it slow or juvenile. You will know within the free preview which camp you are in.

PM PrepCast is the opposite temperament. It is structured, lecture-based video from an instructor with a long track record, closer to a traditional course moved online. If you have done well with video lectures before and you want to move at your own speed without characters and animations, it will feel more direct. The tradeoff is that a lecture is a lecture: it asks less of you moment to moment, so staying engaged is on you.

Neither approach is better in the abstract. Be honest about which one matches how you actually study, because the best course is the one you will finish.

Question banks and mock exams

On raw question count, the two are close: Brain Sensei advertises 2,200+ and PM PrepCast's Elite simulator has 1,920. That gap is smaller than it looks, because most candidates work through 800 to 1,500 questions in a normal prep window, so both banks are deep enough.

Where PM PrepCast has a real edge is full-length mock exams. Its simulator ships four complete 180-question exams, and the PMP is a stamina test as much as a knowledge test. Sitting for a full timed exam more than once is genuinely useful conditioning, not just knowledge checking. If multiple full-length dry runs matter to you, that is a point for PrepCast.

The thing to watch with both is variety over depth. A number on a sales page tells you how many questions exist, not how many are distinct or how closely they mirror the 2026 formats. This is the same reason many candidates end up pairing any course with a second, dedicated question bank in the final stretch, which is the next section.

Pricing in real terms

The headline prices land in the same neighborhood, around $349 for a year of the full thing, but the model is different and that changes the math.

PM PrepCast is a one-time payment. You buy it, you own the course access for a year, done. There is no clock pressure from a subscription, and if you study slowly it costs the same. Brain Sensei's annual plan is similar in spirit, but its monthly $99.99 option can quietly get expensive: three months is about $300, six months is about $600. The monthly plan rewards fast finishers and punishes slow ones. If you go Brain Sensei and expect a longer runway, take the annual, not the monthly.

One more real-terms note. Both prices are before the discounts these providers run constantly. Check for a current coupon before you check out, on either one.

Which one fits you

Pick Brain Sensei if you know you disengage from standard video, you want the material to be interactive and memorable, you like the safety of a pass guarantee, and you will finish fast enough that the pricing works in your favor.

Pick The PM PrepCast if you learn well from structured video lectures, you want a one-time payment with no subscription clock, you value multiple full-length mock exams for stamina practice, and you like buying from a long-established name.

If you are still torn, both offer a free preview. Ten minutes inside each one tells you more about the teaching style, which is the real decision here, than any review can.

Common Questions

Is Brain Sensei or PM PrepCast better for the 2026 PMP exam?
Neither is universally better. They do the same job, teach you the material and give you the 35 contact hours, but in different styles. Brain Sensei uses an animated, story-based format and sells on a subscription. PM PrepCast uses lecture-style video, is a one-time payment, and ships more full-length mock exams. Pick Brain Sensei if you learn best when the material is engaging and interactive. Pick PM PrepCast if you prefer straight video lessons and a bigger bank of timed practice exams.

Is Brain Sensei or PM PrepCast a PMI Authorized Training Partner?
Neither one is. PMI replaced its Registered Education Provider program with the Authorized Training Partner (ATP) program in 2021, and the ATP program does not accept self-paced online courses. Both were affected, so neither carries the ATP badge. That does not matter for eligibility. You do not need an ATP course, and both Brain Sensei and PM PrepCast still issue a valid 35-contact-hour certificate that satisfies PMI's education requirement.

How much do Brain Sensei and PM PrepCast cost in 2026?
As of mid-2026, Brain Sensei is around $349.99 for a year of access or $99.99 per month. PM PrepCast Elite, which bundles the course and the exam simulator, is around $349 as a one-time payment, and the course-only Basic tier is around $279. Both providers run frequent discounts, so check the current price at checkout before you buy.

Do I need a separate question bank with Brain Sensei or PM PrepCast?
Both include practice questions, so you can pass with either one alone. Many candidates still add a dedicated question bank in the final weeks for more volume, fresh questions, and per-topic feedback. PM Mastery is one option: 4,500+ questions tagged to the 2026 ECO with an AI Coach that explains every answer. It does not provide contact hours, so it is a practice add-on, not a replacement for the course.
