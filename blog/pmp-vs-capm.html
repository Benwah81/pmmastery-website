CAPM vs PMP (2026): Which Should You Get First?

2026 eligibility, PMI's current exam fees, and the CAPM-to-PMP path, side by side.

If you lead projects and meet the PMP experience requirement, get the PMP. If you don't meet it yet, the CAPM is the entry-level option, and an active CAPM covers the PMP's 35 contact hours when you're ready to apply. The PMP needs 24 to 60 months of experience leading projects in the last 10 years, depending on your education. The CAPM needs no experience, just a secondary degree and 23 hours of project management education.

Both certifications come from PMI. Everything below reflects the PMP exam that launched on July 9, 2026, and PMI's certification pages as checked on October 28, 2026.

The Quick Answer

Get the PMP if: you've led projects long enough to meet your experience tier (36 months with a bachelor's degree, for example) and want the credential employers ask for by name. If you qualify, go straight for it.

Get the CAPM if: you're new to project management, don't meet the PMP experience requirement yet, or work on projects without leading them.

Eligibility Requirements

PMP Requirements (2026)

Under the July 2026 rules, you need one of four education and experience combinations. The experience is non-overlapping months spent leading projects in a professional setting, all within the last 10 years.

  • Secondary school (high school diploma, GED, or equivalent): 60 months
  • Associate's degree or equivalent (EQF Level 5 / ISCED 5): 48 months
  • Bachelor's degree or higher: 36 months
  • Bachelor's or postgraduate degree from a GAC-accredited program: 24 months

Every path also needs 35 contact hours of project management education. An active CAPM waives that requirement, and coursework from a GAC-accredited degree can count toward it.

Key point: the experience has to be leading and directing projects. School projects, personal events, and routine operational work don't count. The full breakdown, including how PMI counts overlapping projects, is in our 2026 PMP requirements guide.

CAPM Requirements

  • Secondary degree (high school diploma, GED, or global equivalent)
  • At least 18 years old
  • At least 23 hours of project management education completed before the exam
  • No project management experience required

That opens the CAPM to students, career changers, and people who work on projects without leading them.

Going From CAPM to PMP

This is the part most comparisons get wrong. PMI's July 2026 PMP Exam Content Outline says an active CAPM counts as a 35-hour training credit, so you don't document contact hours on your PMP application. The CAPM has to still be active when you apply.

What the CAPM doesn't do is shorten the experience requirement. You still need the 24 to 60 months that match your education.

The path looks like this:

  1. Earn the CAPM (23 hours of education, 150-question exam).
  2. Keep leading projects and log your months as you go.
  3. Renew the CAPM with 15 PDUs if your 3-year cycle ends before you qualify.
  4. Apply for the PMP once your experience reaches your tier, with the 35-hour requirement already covered.

You pay for two exams, but you don't need a separate 35-hour course. If you already qualify for the PMP, the CAPM step adds an exam fee and a few months of study for no gain.

Exam Difficulty

PMP Exam

The PMP is the harder of the two, and the 2026 version tests judgment more than recall:

  • Situational questions: most describe a project situation and ask what you'd do next. There's rarely a textbook answer to look up.
  • Wide scope: predictive, agile, and hybrid approaches across three domains, weighted People 33%, Process 41%, and Business Environment 26% since July 2026.
  • Endurance: 180 questions in 240 minutes, with two 10-minute breaks. You can't go back to a section after its break.

Typical study time: 150-200 hours over 2-3 months

More on what changed in July 2026 in Is the PMP exam harder in 2026?

CAPM Exam

PMI describes the CAPM as covering predictive project management, agile principles, and business analysis. Compared with the PMP:

  • Foundational focus: more of the exam checks whether you know the concepts and terms, less of it tests calls in messy situations.
  • Shorter: 150 questions in 180 minutes.

Typical study time: 75-100 hours over 6-8 weeks

The Bottom Line on Difficulty

The PMP is harder, but real project experience helps a lot. Many scenario questions are easier when you've lived through something similar.

The CAPM is easier on paper, but it can be tough if you're learning project management concepts without real-world context to anchor them.

Career and Salary Impact

PMP Career Impact

  • Salary: PMI reports that PMP holders have a 17% higher median salary than people without the certification, and puts the US median for PMP holders at $135,000.
  • Reach: PMI says more than 1.7 million people hold the PMP, which makes it the most widely held project management certification.
  • Career advancement: often listed as required or preferred for senior PM, PM director, and consulting roles.

CAPM Career Impact

  • Salary: PMI doesn't publish a CAPM salary figure from its own survey, so we're not quoting one. Salary sites mix countries and job titles, which is why their CAPM numbers swing so widely.
  • Best for: landing a first role such as project coordinator, project analyst, or assistant project manager.
  • Limits: rarely enough on its own for senior roles, and often treated as a stepping stone to the PMP.

The PMP opens more doors. The CAPM can help you get through the first one.

How to Decide: A Simple Framework

Get the PMP if:

  • You meet your experience tier (24 to 60 months leading projects in the last 10 years)
  • You're already working as a project manager
  • You want the bigger career and salary payoff
  • You're applying for senior PM roles
  • Your employer will pay for it (common for PMP, less so for CAPM)

Get the CAPM if:

  • You don't have enough experience for the PMP yet
  • You're a student or recent graduate interested in project management
  • You're moving into project management from another field
  • You work on projects but don't lead them
  • You want the 35-hour credit in hand before you apply for the PMP

Skip the CAPM and wait for the PMP if:

  • You're 6-12 months away from meeting your experience tier
  • You're already in a PM role gaining experience
  • Budget is tight (save for one exam, not two)

Common Questions

Does an active CAPM waive the PMP's 35 contact hours?

Yes. PMI's July 2026 PMP Exam Content Outline says an active CAPM counts as a 35-hour training credit, so you don't document contact hours on your PMP application. It doesn't reduce the project experience you need.

Is it worth getting the CAPM before the PMP?

It is if you're more than a year from meeting the PMP experience requirement, since it gives you a credential now and covers the 35 hours later. If you already qualify or you're close, go straight to the PMP and skip the second exam fee.

Which is harder to maintain, the PMP or the CAPM?

The PMP. PMP holders need 60 PDUs every 3 years, while CAPM holders need 15 PDUs every 3 years.

What happens if I fail the PMP or CAPM exam?

You can take the exam up to three times within your 1-year eligibility period, and you pay a fee again for the second and third attempts. After three failed attempts, you wait one year from your last exam before you can reapply for that certification.

The Bottom Line

The PMP is the better investment if you qualify. It costs more and takes more preparation, and it's the one employers ask for by name.

The CAPM fits a specific situation: you're new to project management or still building your months. Since an active CAPM covers the PMP's 35 hours, it isn't wasted effort if the PMP is the goal.

Don't get the CAPM just because it seems easier. Get it because it fits where you are right now.
