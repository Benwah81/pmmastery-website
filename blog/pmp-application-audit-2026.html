The PMP Application, Section by Section, and What Happens in an Audit

September 27, 2026 8 min read Application, Audit, Eligibility

The PMP® application is five sections and an agreement, and most of the work sits in one of them: the experience entries. Every application can be audited, and PMI® can audit you even after you're certified. So the safe way to fill it out is also the simple one: write down only what you actually did, month by month, in words your supervisor would sign. Here's what each section asks for as of September 2026, what PMI requests in an audit, and what to have ready before you submit.

Everything below comes from PMI's own material: the PMP certification page, the July 2026 Exam Content Outline, the Certification Handbook (revised June 2026), PMI's certification agreement, and the live application form as it looked in September 2026. If you're still working out whether you qualify, start with our 2026 PMP requirements guide. It covers the four education paths and the 35 contact hours, so this post doesn't repeat them.

What to Have Ready Before You Start

The application goes faster, and holds up better, if you gather these first:

  • Your education details. Start and end year, and the institution's full name. The form asks you to avoid abbreviations and acronyms.
  • A list of the projects you led in the last 10 years, with the start and end month of each.
  • A name for each project. The supervisor or manager who could confirm your role, and a way to reach them now, not the way you reached them three jobs ago.
  • Your 35-hour training record. The provider, the course title, the hours, and the certificate or letter that proves it.

The Application, Section by Section

PMI's form walks you through these in order. Here's what each one asks for and where people tend to slip.

Section What it asks for Where people slip
Academic Education Country or region, highest level of education from a dropdown, start and end year, institution name Abbreviating the school's name
Project Management Experience Per entry: organization, job title, a "currently in this project" box, start and end month, and a Project Summary of 100 to 500 words One entry for a whole job, routine operations work, overlapping months counted twice
Project Professional Training Live instructor-led or self-paced, training organization, course title, hours completed Listing books or practice exams, which PMI doesn't accept as training on their own
Application Review A summary of everything you entered Skimming it instead of checking every date
Certification Profile Details Exam country, citizenship, your name as it appears on your ID, date of birth, address, phone, the name for your certificate, accommodations Typing a name that doesn't match your ID
Agreement and submit A checkbox accepting PMI's certification agreement Ticking it without reading what it says

That's the form as of September 2026. PMI changes it from time to time, so trust the live form over any guide, this one included.

The Experience Section, Where Most of the Work Is

PMI wants experience leading and directing projects, in a professional setting, within the 10 years before you submit. It doesn't have to be paid. School projects, research for a degree, planning personal events and home improvement don't count, and the Exam Content Outline says your entries shouldn't include routine, operational or administrative tasks. A project has a beginning, an end and a result. Running a help desk doesn't.

Each entry gets one Project Summary box. PMI's helper text asks you to summarize your role, the project objective, your contributions, and the outcomes, in 100 to 500 words. There are no separate fields for team size, budget or methodology. If those matter to the story, they go in the summary.

How PMI counts the months

PMI counts experience in months, not hours or projects, and both the start and end month count. January 2020 to September 2026 is 81 months. If you tick "currently in this project," the current month counts. Months where projects overlap count once: two projects that both ran January to March 2020 give you 3 months, not 6, and PMI's own example of one project from January to March and another from February to May comes to 5. Your total has to reach 60, 48, 36 or 24 months depending on your education path.

One project per entry

The form asks for an organization and a job title, so it's tempting to write one entry for a whole job. Don't. PMI's certification page says you'll list each project separately on the PMP application, and the Exam Content Outline says all project experience must be recorded individually. A single project is also easier for a supervisor to recognize and sign.

What You Agree To When You Submit

The last step is a checkbox accepting PMI's certification agreement. In plain English, and this isn't legal advice:

  • Everything has to be true and complete. Inaccurate answers can lead to an investigation or sanctions even if the mistake wasn't intentional.
  • PMI can check. It can contact people and organizations to confirm what you wrote, not just during an audit.
  • PMI decides. Whether you qualify is PMI's call, and it's final.

Submitting also means you agree to comply with PMI's audit process. That's the practical reason not to pad a single month or stretch a role. Copying someone else's example entry fails the same test: it isn't a description of your work.

How the PMP Audit Works

This is all from PMI's Certification Handbook, revised June 2026, pages 5 and 6.

  • Who gets audited. Every application is subject to audit, and a percentage are selected at random. PMI also audits every application from its employees and volunteers, and it can select any candidate at any time, including after certification. Being picked isn't a sign something looked wrong.
  • When you find out. By email, before you pay the certification fee.
  • What PMI asks for. Copies of your diploma or degree, signatures from your supervisors or managers for the experience you recorded, and certificates or letters from the training providers for each course you listed toward the 35 hours.
  • How long you have. 90 days to submit the documents. Your audit package is on your mypmi.org dashboard. Once complete documentation is in, PMI says the audit takes about 5 to 7 business days.
  • What pauses. You can't continue with certification until the audit is done. Your one-year exam eligibility period starts the day PMI tells you that you've completed it.
  • If it fails. If you don't meet the requirements or don't respond, the audit fails and PMI enforces a one-year suspension before you can reapply. If you fail an audit after you're certified, there's no refund. A failed audit can be appealed in writing within 30 days (handbook page 18).

The July 2026 Exam Content Outline also says PMI is introducing enhanced verification within the PMP application system. PMI hasn't published details of what that involves.

Getting Ready Before You Submit, Not After

Nothing makes an application immune to audit, and nothing should try to. What you control is whether an audit is a paperwork week or a problem. Do these before you click submit:

  • Write each entry for the person who'll sign it. Your role, not the team's, in words they'd recognize. If your supervisor would hesitate to sign it as written, the entry needs fixing.
  • Confirm you can reach each supervisor now. People change jobs. Finding someone three months into a 90-day window is the hard way.
  • Check the months. Nothing counted twice across entries, nothing older than 10 years, and the dates match your records.
  • Have the files in hand. Your diploma or degree and your training certificates, saved somewhere you'll find them.
  • Keep a copy of exactly what you submitted. Every summary and every date. An audit can come after certification too.

After You're Approved

Once your application is accepted, you pay the exam fee and schedule the exam. PMI's handbook gives you a one-year eligibility period and up to three attempts inside it. If you were audited, the year starts when PMI tells you the audit is complete. For the current fee, check PMI's certification page; our requirements guide notes what's changed this year.

Common Questions

How does the PMP audit work?
PMI selects a percentage of applications at random and tells you by email before you pay the exam fee. You then have 90 days to send proof of your education, supervisor or manager signatures for the experience you recorded, and certificates for your 35 contact hours. Once complete documentation is in, PMI says the audit takes about 5 to 7 business days.

What documents do I need for a PMP audit?
Copies of your diploma or degree, signatures from the supervisors or managers for the experience you recorded, and certificates or letters from each training provider you listed for your 35 hours. Your audit package is on your mypmi.org dashboard, and PMI's audit email explains the steps.

What happens if you fail the PMP audit?
If you don't meet the audit requirements or don't respond, the audit fails and PMI enforces a one-year suspension before you can reapply. A failed audit can be appealed in writing within 30 days, per PMI's certification handbook.

Can PMI audit you after you're certified?
Yes. Selection is random, but PMI's handbook says it can audit any candidate at any time, including after certification, and there's no refund if you fail an audit after you're certified. Keep a copy of what you submitted and your documents for that reason.
